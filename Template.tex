\epigraph{The best way to predict the future is to invent it.}{--- Alan Kay}

\begin{thesis}
\textbf{In one paragraph:} State the core argument here. What do you believe that
others might not? Why does it matter now?
\end{thesis}

\section{The Observation}
Start with something concrete: a trend you've noticed, a story, a number that
surprised you. Ground the reader before you zoom out.

\section{Why It's Happening}
Explain the forces underneath. Technology, demographics, incentives, culture.
\aside{Tip: one strong cause beats five weak ones.}

\begin{keyidea}
The single sentence you want the reader to remember from this section.
\end{keyidea}

\section{Where This Leads}
Play the trend forward. Be specific enough to be wrong.

\prediction{Something you expect to happen by 2030.}{70}
\prediction{A bolder, less certain call.}{35}

\pullquote{A line worth quoting on its own.}

\section{Opportunities}
What becomes possible, or necessary, if you're right?

\begin{bizidea}{Idea Name}
\field{Problem}{Who is hurting, and how badly?}
\field{Insight}{What do you see that incumbents don't?}
\field{Solution}{What would you build?}
\field{Why now}{What changed that makes this possible today?}
\field{Model}{How does it make money?}
\field{Risks}{What would kill it?}
\field{First step}{The cheapest test you could run this month.}
\end{bizidea}

\section{Counterarguments}
Steelman the other side. Where might you be wrong?

\section{What I'm Going to Do}
Close with a personal takeaway or decision, what changes for you, not just
for ``the world.''
